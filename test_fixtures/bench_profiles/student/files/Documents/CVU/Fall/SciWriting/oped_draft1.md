The Pipes Under Our Feet
by Talia Brennan

Every summer at my grandmother's house in Milwaukee, I let the kitchen tap run for thirty seconds before filling a glass. Nobody ever told me why. Years later I learned that the line bringing water into her house was made of lead.

Across the Midwest, hundreds of thousands of homes still get their water through lead service lines. Replacing one costs between $5,000 and $12,000. [Fairweather: why should the reader care about this number?]

Renters are the people with the least say. A landlord decides whether to replace a line; a tenant just drinks the water. [Fairweather: this is your argument, move it up.]

Cities should require landlords to disclose lead lines in every lease, and should pay for replacement first in rental buildings.
